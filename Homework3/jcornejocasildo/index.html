Exploring how Mental Health Concerns Vary Across Students' Year of Study

Diving into the Dashboard

This dashboard explores reported mental-health conditions amongst students such as depression, anxiety, panic attacks, and treatment sought amongst students of different academic years. Throughout their academic careers, students can face high pressure situations when faced with difficult classes, responsibilities, and navigating one's adult life. Therefore being able to see how many students are impacted by mental health concerns and not receiving professional treatment can allow us to reflect on how well students are being supported and offered resources during a very important and challenging time in their academic career.

About the Dataset

The data presented in this visualization dashboard was gathered from "Student Mental Health Dataset" which gathered information about university students mental and overall well-being. This data was collected through the use of anonymous surveys, but each survey response included students' self-reported information regarding their gender, year of study, mental health condition, and whether or not they sought professional help for treatment.

Overview: Mental Health Concerns by Year of Study

What this graph shows

This bar chart compares the percentage of students in each academic year who reported depression, anxiety, or panic attacks. This helps give us an overview of the distribution of students from each academic year that reported such conditions.

Hovering over each bar shows the exact percentage and number of students represented. Please refer to the legend below the chart to idenitfy the different mental health conditions represented in the chart.

Focus: Mental Health Patterns by Academic Year

What this graph shows

The heatmap shows how mental health concerns and treatment-seeking vary across academic years.

Hovering over each cell will show the exact percentage and the year of the students who reported having that mental meath concern of seeking treatment. Darker cells represent higher percentages of students who answered “Yes” for that category, while lighter colored cells represent a lower percentages of the students who answered "Yes for that category."

Advanced: Academic Year, Mental Health Concern, and Treatment Seeking

What this graph shows

The Sankey diagram shows how students from different academic years move, to whether they reported a mental health concern, to whether they sought treatment. Students were reported to have a mental health concern if they reported having one or more of the mental-health conditions of depression, anxiety, or having experienced panic attacks.

As we see the flow from left to right on our diagram we see that a majority of students were found to have a mental health concern but an overwhelming minority of students actuall sought professional treatment.
